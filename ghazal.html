ये भी ठीक है

Ye Bhi Theek Hai

An Original Ghazal — Emotion: Resigned Acceptance

The Complete Ghazal

Technical Structure

Element Detail
Emotion Resigned acceptance — the quiet "this too is fine" you say when nothing is fine
Radif ये भी ठीक है (ye bhi theek hai — this too is fine)
Qafiya -आया (-aaya): जगाया, आया, दिखाया, लगाया, रुलाया, छुपाया, सुनाया, सिखाया, सजाया
Shers 8 (matla + 6 interior + maqta)
Matla Both lines end with qafiya + radif ✓
Language Conversational Hindi — no heavy Urdu vocabulary

The Unified Story

This ghazal tells one continuous story: a person who loved deeply, was betrayed, tried to heal but couldn't escape the memories, drowned the days in distraction but was ambushed by the silence of night, learned to hide an ocean from the world, was haunted by the beloved's fragrance in every breeze, was taught by loneliness itself how to walk alone, and finally — after falling again and again — rose each time and adorned themselves. "Ye bhi theek hai" evolves from helpless resignation into quiet, defiant resilience.

1
The Sleepless Night: Pain arrives → memories of the beloved attack in sleep → first quiet acceptance
2
The Betrayal: Invested everything in relationships → everyone showed their true face → accepts this too
3
The Failed Cure: People promised time would heal → time rubbed salt on wounds instead → accepts even this
4
Day vs. Night: Drowns in daytime distractions → night's silence strips everything away and makes you cry → nowhere to hide from yourself
5
The Hidden Ocean: World asks "how are you?" → says "fine" → hides an entire ocean behind the eyes
6
The Phantom Fragrance: The beloved's shadow left but the fragrance stayed → every breeze delivers their message → the senses won't let go
7
The Lonely Teacher: Everyone left → loneliness itself became the teacher → learned to walk alone
8
Rising After Every Fall: Fell again and again → but rose each time → adorned self after every wound → "ye bhi theek hai" becomes a battle cry, not a surrender

Sher-by-Sher Annotation

Sher 1 — Matla (Opening)

MATLA दर्द ने आके रात भर जगाया, ये भी ठीक है
नींद में फिर तेरा ख़याल आया, ये भी ठीक है Dard ne aake raat bhar jagaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Neend mein phir tera khayaal aaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: Pain came and kept me awake all night — that's fine too. In my sleep, your thought came to me again — that's fine too.

Grammar: "दर्द ने" — pain + ergative marker, correct with transitive past "जगाया" (kept awake). "रात भर" — all night. "नींद में" — in sleep. "तेरा ख़याल" — your thought (masculine), correctly matched with "आया" (masculine past).

Imagery: Pain is personified — it arrives like an unwanted guest. Then in the brief sleep that follows, the beloved's memory slips in. Two attacks in one night, both met with a shrug.

Sher 2 — The Betrayal

2 हमने हर रिश्ता बहुत जी लगा के रखा था
फिर भी एक दिन सबने अपना चेहरा दिखाया, ये भी ठीक है Humne har rishta bahut jee lagaa ke rakha tha
Phir bhi ek din sabne apna chehra dikhaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: We kept every relationship alive with all our heart. Yet one day, everyone showed their true face — that's fine too.

Grammar: "हमने" + transitive "रखा था" (had kept) ✓. "सबने" + transitive "दिखाया" (showed) ✓. The idiom "चेहरा दिखाना" (to reveal one's true colors) is used correctly.

Story connection: After the sleepless night of Sher 1, we now learn why — it wasn't just one person. Every relationship was nurtured with devotion, and every one betrayed. The scope widens from personal to universal.

Sher 3 — The Failed Cure (Improved)

3 — IMPROVED कहते हैं लोग कि वक़्त हर दर्द भुला देता है
वक़्त ने ख़ुद ही नमक ज़ख़्मों पे लगाया, ये भी ठीक है Kehte hain log ki waqt har dard bhula deta hai
Waqt ne khud hi namak zakhmon pe lagaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: People say time makes you forget every pain. Time itself rubbed salt on the wounds — that's fine too.

Grammar: "कहते हैं" — present habitual, masculine plural matching "लोग" ✓. "वक़्त ने" — time + ergative ✓. "नमक" (salt, masculine) + "लगाया" (masculine singular) ✓. "ज़ख़्मों पे" — on wounds (oblique plural) ✓.

What changed from the original: The previous version ("वक़्त ने उल्टा हमें और भी रुलाया") was too blunt and conversational — "उल्टा" (on the contrary) lacked poetic weight, and "और भी रुलाया" (made us cry more) was vague. The new version replaces this with a visceral physical image: "नमक ज़ख़्मों पे लगाना" — rubbing salt on wounds. Time isn't just failing to heal — it's actively making the wounds burn. The image is immediate, specific, and universally understood.

Story connection: After betrayal, the speaker tried the universal remedy — waiting for time to heal. But time turned traitor too.

Sher 4 — Day vs. Night (New)

4 — NEW दिन भर ख़ुद को तमाशों में बहलाया हमने
रात आई तो सन्नाटे ने रुलाया, ये भी ठीक है Din bhar khud ko tamaashon mein behlaaya humne
Raat aayi to sannaate ne rulaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: All day we distracted ourselves with spectacles and diversions. When night came, the silence made us cry — that's fine too.

Grammar: "हमने" + transitive "बहलाया" (distracted) ✓. "तमाशों में" — in spectacles/diversions (oblique plural) ✓. "रात आई" — "रात" (night, feminine) + "आई" (feminine past) ✓. "सन्नाटे ने" — silence (masculine oblique before ने) + transitive "रुलाया" (made cry, masculine) ✓.

Imagery: "तमाशे" (spectacles) — not just work or busyness, but the speaker drowns in the noise and theater of life as a deliberate distraction. Then night strips away all performance. Silence is personified as an aggressor — it doesn't just surround you, it makes you cry. The contrast between daytime noise and nighttime silence is visceral: you can deceive yourself during the day, but the night knows the truth.

Story connection: After time rubbed salt on wounds (Sher 3), the speaker tries a new strategy — drowning in daytime distractions. But the night is honest and merciless.

Sher 5 — The Hidden Ocean

5 दुनिया पूछे तो कह देते हैं सब अच्छा है
आँखों में जो समंदर था वो छुपाया, ये भी ठीक है Duniya poochhe to keh dete hain sab achchha hai
Aankhon mein jo samandar tha wo chhupaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: When the world asks, I say everything is fine. The ocean that lived in my eyes — I hid that too. That's fine.

Grammar: "दुनिया पूछे तो" — conditional subjunctive ✓. "कह देते हैं" — habitual present, first person formal ✓. "आँखों में जो समंदर था" — relative clause: "समंदर" (ocean, masculine) + "था" (masculine past) ✓. "वो छुपाया" — "वो" refers to "समंदर" (masculine) + "छुपाया" (masculine) ✓.

Imagery: This is the mask sher. The world runs on "how are you?" and the speaker says "fine." But behind the eyes lives an entire ocean — not just tears, an ocean. And they hide it. The scale is what makes this devastating: you're not hiding a tear, you're hiding a sea.

Story connection: After trying time (failed) and distance (failed), the speaker adopts a third strategy: concealment. They wear a mask for the world. The pain doesn't shrink — it becomes an ocean. But no one sees it.

Sher 6 — The Phantom Fragrance (New)

6 — NEW तेरा साया तो गया पर तेरी ख़ुशबू रह गई
हर हवा ने तेरा पैग़ाम सुनाया, ये भी ठीक है Tera saaya to gaya par teri khushboo reh gayi
Har hawa ne tera paighaam sunaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: Your shadow left but your fragrance remained. Every breeze delivered your message — that's fine too.

Grammar: "तेरा साया" (your shadow, masculine) + "गया" (masculine past) ✓. "तेरी ख़ुशबू" (your fragrance, feminine) + "रह गई" (feminine past) ✓. "हर हवा ने" — every breeze + ergative ✓. "तेरा पैग़ाम" (your message, masculine) + "सुनाया" (masculine past) ✓.

Imagery: The beloved is physically gone — their shadow (the visible, tangible form) has departed. But the fragrance (the invisible, sensory essence) persists. This is devastatingly specific: you can close your eyes against a shadow, but you cannot stop breathing. And now every breeze becomes a messenger — the air itself is an accomplice of memory. The senses conspire against forgetting.

Story connection: After hiding the ocean from the world (Sher 5), the speaker discovers that the beloved's presence has seeped into the atmosphere itself. You can hide your pain from people, but you can't hide from the wind.

Sher 7 — The Lonely Teacher (New)

7 — NEW जब सभी साथ छोड़ गए तो ख़ुद से कहा
अकेलेपन ने ही चलना हमको सिखाया, ये भी ठीक है Jab sabhi saath chhor gaye to khud se kaha
Akelepan ne hi chalna humko sikhaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: When everyone left, I told myself: loneliness itself taught me how to walk — that's fine too.

Grammar: "सभी" + intransitive "छोड़ गए" (left, masculine plural) ✓. "ख़ुद से कहा" — said to oneself ✓. "अकेलेपन ने" — loneliness (masculine oblique before ने) + transitive "सिखाया" (taught) ✓. "हमको" — dative, us ✓.

Imagery: A paradox — the very thing that should have paralyzed the speaker (loneliness) becomes the teacher. Loneliness personified as a harsh but transformative mentor. "चलना सिखाया" (taught to walk) evokes a child learning to walk — the speaker is reborn through abandonment. This is the pivot point of the ghazal: from suffering to growth.

Story connection: The tone shifts here. After 6 shers of loss, silence, hiding, and haunting — the speaker begins to find something in the loneliness. Not happiness, not healing, but capability. The acceptance begins to transform.

Sher 8 — Maqta (Closing)

MAQTA गिरे बार बार पर हर बार उठ खड़े हुए
हमने हर ज़ख़्म के बाद ख़ुद को सजाया, ये भी ठीक है Gire baar baar par har baar uth khade hue
Humne har zakhm ke baad khud ko sajaaya, ye bhi theek hai
Translation: We fell again and again, but stood up each time. After every wound, we adorned ourselves — that's fine too.

Grammar: "गिरे" (fell, masculine plural) — intransitive, no ने ✓. "उठ खड़े हुए" (stood up, masculine plural) ✓. "हमने" + transitive "सजाया" (adorned, masculine matching ख़ुद) ✓. "हर ज़ख़्म के बाद" — after every wound (oblique) ✓.

Imagery: The most defiant image of the ghazal. "सजाना" (to adorn, to beautify) after a wound — not just surviving, not just standing up, but making yourself beautiful again. After every wound, the speaker doesn't merely endure — they decorate themselves. This is grace under fire. The image transforms "ये भी ठीक है" from its original resignation into something luminous: it is no longer a sigh — it is a declaration. Everything that happened? Fine. Because I'm still standing, still adorned, still alive.

Story connection: This is the culmination. Sleepless nights (1) → betrayal (2) → time failing (3) → night's silence (4) → the mask (5) → phantom fragrance (6) → loneliness as teacher (7) → and finally, the rising. The ghazal that began in pain ends in defiance. "Ye bhi theek hai" — said one last time, not with defeat but with the quiet power of someone who has been through everything and chosen to bloom anyway.

What Changed & Why

Sher Status Reason
1 (Matla) Kept as-is ✓ Strong opening — pain personified, beloved's memory in sleep. Clean grammar. No changes needed.
2 Kept as-is ✓ Universal betrayal theme. "Chehra dikhaana" idiom works perfectly. Clean grammar.
3 Improved ✓ Replaced vague "ulta rulaaya" with visceral "namak zakhmon pe lagaaya" (salt on wounds). Same idea, much stronger image.
4 Replaced ✓ Old sher (gali/mod — streets and turns) had flat imagery. New sher: daytime distractions vs. night's silence — silence personified as an aggressor. Much more visceral contrast.
5 Kept as-is ✓ "Ocean behind the eyes" — powerful scale of hidden pain. The mask the speaker wears for the world.
6 New ✓ Phantom fragrance: beloved's shadow left but scent remained, every breeze delivers their message. Sensory imagery — you can't stop breathing.
7 New ✓ Loneliness as teacher: the paradox of abandonment becoming a mentor. Pivot from suffering to growth. "Taught to walk" evokes rebirth.
8 (Maqta) Replaced ✓ Old maqta (broken pieces bowing) was too passive. New maqta: falling and rising, adorning yourself after every wound — defiance, not defeat. "Ye bhi theek hai" transforms from a sigh into a battle cry.
